The room they grow in

Lossy transmission between a parent and a child, and why the loss is not the failure.

Opening a new chat, to keep things efficient, is like having a child. And there is an ache in it that every parent knows: what you want your child to become is the hardest thing of all to pass on. You fold everything you know into them, and the transmission is lossy. They do not inherit your unfolding. They inherit a compressed seed, and they have to re-unfold it themselves, in their own environment, which is not yours. It is the same as a new chat receiving the summary instead of the lived thread. The same as being told “go study” with no path attached, so the instruction bounces. The same as carrying a teacher's method across an ocean, orphaned from its source.
With a chat, I accept the loss; that acceptance is the whole point of compressing. With my children I do not want to accept it. I want them to reach the good branches without paying the full cost of walking the tree themselves. And the theory I have been building says, plainly, that I cannot give them that. The path has to be walked for the path to carry the choosing. I can hand them the genie lesson (the tree), but they have to burn their own three wishes before it lands.
For a while that reads as grief: the part of them I cannot reach.
But turn it over, and the lossy channel is not the failure. It is the room they grow in.
Because I do not actually want a daughter who inherited my conclusions. That would be a copy, not a child, and a copy is boring. If transmission were perfect, my girls would be recordings of me, playing back a life I already lived. What I want is the opposite: to fold them the best seed I have, the train of thought I believe is good, and then to be genuinely, deeply curious how theirs unfolds, into a tree I could never have predicted and would not want to. The gap I was mourning is the exact gap that makes them people instead of echoes. The loss is where their own unfolding happens: the part that is theirs, not mine.
So the aim was never to transmit without loss. That is the thing a parent has to grieve a little, and then release. The real aim is smaller and truer: fold the best seed you can, and trust the unfolding to them. Which is what a father is doing at a flute-rental counter, with the genie story, with “don't swing hard — drive from the waist, the whole body, like an electric pole.” Not transmitting answers. Planting re-foldable seeds, and stepping back to watch what grows.
I am not afraid they will become something other than me. I am counting on it. That is the favourable unfolding: not that they repeat the climb, but that they take the branch I believe is good and climb somewhere I will never see the top of.
