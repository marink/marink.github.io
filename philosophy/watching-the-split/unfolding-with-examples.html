Unfolding with examples

Why clear to me is not the test, why unfolding has no free bottom, and the examples that break the loop.

Clear to me is not the test

Reading this writing back, each part felt clear to me, and of course it did: I already hold the folded statements. “I understand everything” is not the test. Whether you do is. A sentence still in its folded state reads as cryptic, not deep, to anyone who lacks the key, and if the reader cannot unfold it, the writing has defeated its own purpose. So the folds have to open on your side too: some compression traded back for reach.

Unfolding has no free bottom

But unfolding is not free. When you unfold a sentence you add prose, and you pretend the added words are simple. They are not: each word has its own folding inside it. Fail to see that and you fall into an unending, tedious loop, glossing every word of every gloss, with no bottom.
Being aware of that is the lever. It lets you stop at the right depth instead of looping: unfold enough to open the fold, not infinitely.

Unfold with what the reader already holds

That is the reasoning behind a strategy, not an excuse to stay cryptic. Do not pile on more prose that needs its own unfolding. Unfold with examples that already live, folded, in the reader's own experience. An example is a fold the reader can open because they already hold the key: I point at something you have lived, and your experience does the unfolding. It breaks the loop, because the work is handed to what you already carry.

The first example: wireless charging

Set a phone on a wireless charging pad: no cable, no contact. Inside the pad, a coil drives a magnetic field that keeps reversing; across the gap, a coil in the phone answers it with a current of its own, moving in its own way, and energy crosses without anything being poured from one to the other.
That is how I picture what this writing tries to do. The pad is me showing my way; the phone is you. There is no wire: I do not pour content into you. Your own coil has to turn to receive it. Induction, not transmission. Showing you my way is meant to give you energy for your unfolding, in whatever part of your life it applies to.

What the example is not

It is not a picture of thought against matter, or of the one and the not. It is simply how I conceptualise the transfer, and a clean picture of the whole method: re-derived, not transmitted.

The catch: scripture already does this

I arrived at “teach with examples” myself, and then noticed that the Bible has been doing it all along. It teaches through passages that work as examples, and the Ten Commandments are the live illustration: law folded small enough to be remembered, carried, and unfolded by each reader in their own life. So the specimen became a mirror. The form I use to explain is the form my example demonstrates.
The joke works the same way (a joke is a fold): the comedian supplies the setup, and the laugh fires from your own recognition.
